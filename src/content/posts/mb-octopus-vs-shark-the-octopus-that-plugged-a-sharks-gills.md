An octopus has no bones and no shell. So what can it do when a shark has it in its jaws?

## The hunter: the pyjama shark

The pyjama shark is a striped bottom-dweller found off South Africa, in rocky reefs and kelp beds. It is an opportunistic predator that favours cephalopods. Divers have seen pyjama sharks seize octopuses and cuttlefish and tear off their arms with a twisting motion.

## Arms in the gills

In the "Green Seas" episode of the BBC's *Blue Planet II* (2017), filmed off South Africa's southern tip, a pyjama shark seized an octopus. The octopus pushed its arms into the shark's gill slits. With its gills blocked, the shark could not breathe — and it let the octopus go.

## A suit of shells

The shark came back. This time the octopus gathered all the shells it could grab and covered itself with them. The shark was left sniffing around the shell pile while the octopus slipped away.

## Never filmed before

According to the coverage of the series, this shell-armour behaviour had never been recorded before *Blue Planet II* caught it on camera.

## The takeaway

No bones, no shell — so the octopus built one, and nobody had filmed it doing that before.
