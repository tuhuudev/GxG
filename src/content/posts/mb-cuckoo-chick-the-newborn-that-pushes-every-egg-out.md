The common cuckoo never raises its own young. It hands that job to other birds, such as the reed warbler. And the cuckoo chick makes sure it is the only one they raise.

## An egg swapped into the nest

A female cuckoo lays her egg in a host's nest and removes just one of the host's eggs. Taking more could make the hosts abandon the nest altogether.

## Hours old, and already clearing the nest

Shortly after hatching — within about a day, while still naked and blind — the cuckoo chick gets rid of everything else in the nest. It works each host egg or chick onto its back and pushes it up and over the edge, until it is alone.

## One chick that sounds like four

A single chick should get the food of a single chick. But the cuckoo's rapid "si-si-si" begging call sounds like an entire brood of hungry reed warblers. In a 1998 study, Davies, Kilner and Noble played cuckoo begging calls next to single chicks of other species, and the hosts raised their feeding rate to match a cuckoo. The cuckoo is fed as much as a whole brood of four warbler chicks, and for longer.

## A giant in a tiny nest

Fed for a full brood, the cuckoo chick quickly grows far larger than the small warblers caring for it.

## The takeaway

Born blind, the cuckoo chick is raised by the very parents whose eggs it pushed out of the nest.
