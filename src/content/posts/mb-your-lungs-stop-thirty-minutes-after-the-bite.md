# The Bite That Stops Your Lungs

The king cobra is the longest venomous snake on Earth, but its reputation rests on something more specific than size: a single bite can shut down a human being's breathing within about thirty minutes.

## Enough Venom for an Elephant

A king cobra bite is dangerous less because of raw potency than because of sheer volume. One strike can deliver somewhere in the range of 200 to 500 milligrams of venom, and in extreme cases more — a load large enough that it is routinely described as sufficient to kill an elephant. Most venomous snakes simply cannot inject that much in a single bite.

## A Neurotoxin That Cuts the Wire

King cobra venom is predominantly neurotoxic. Its alpha-neurotoxins bind to acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction — the point where a nerve hands its signal to a muscle. Once those receptors are occupied, the signal never arrives. The muscle is intact and the nerve is firing, but nothing connects.

## Why the Eyelids Go First

Neurotoxic envenomation follows a recognisable order. The small muscles controlling the eyelids fail early, producing the drooping lids that clinicians treat as an early warning sign. The paralysis then spreads. The genuinely life-threatening stage is when it reaches the muscles that drive breathing: without ventilation support, respiratory failure can kill within roughly thirty minutes.

## The Snake That Hunts Snakes

Its scientific name, *Ophiophagus hannah*, translates roughly as "snake-eater," and that is exactly what it is. The king cobra preys largely on other snakes — including other king cobras. It is an apex predator whose main prey is the same category of animal that most predators avoid entirely.

That combination is what makes it distinctive: a snake that hunts snakes, carrying enough neurotoxin in one bite to stop a person breathing before help arrives.
