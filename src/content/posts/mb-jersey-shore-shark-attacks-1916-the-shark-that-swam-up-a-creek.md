Between July 1 and July 12, 1916, a series of shark attacks hit the New Jersey coast. Four people died and one survived. The last three attacks didn't happen on a beach at all. They happened in Matawan Creek, roughly eleven miles inland from the ocean, in a town everyone assumed was too far from the sea for a shark to reach.

## A warning nobody believed

Thomas Cottrell, a retired sea captain living in Matawan, reported seeing a shark of about eight feet in the creek. The town dismissed it.

## July 12, around 2 PM

Eleven-year-old Lester Stillwell was swimming in the creek with friends when he was pulled under.

## The rescuer

Stanley Fisher, 24, went into the water to search for the boy. The shark attacked him in front of people on the bank and tore into his thigh. Fisher died of his injuries the same day.

## A third boy

About half an hour later the shark struck again, this time at Joseph Dunn. It clamped onto his leg, and his brother and friends pulled him free in a tug of war with the shark. Dunn was the only victim of the 1916 attacks to survive.

## Which shark?

The species was never confirmed. A bull shark is the leading candidate because bull sharks can swim into fresh and brackish water, but a great white was reportedly caught in the area days later. More than a century later, it is still unproven.

## The takeaway

A shark eleven miles up a creek, and still no one can say for certain which kind it was.
