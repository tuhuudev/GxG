Egg-eating snakes (genus *Dasypeltis*) eat nothing but bird eggs — and they swallow eggs that are much wider than their own heads.

## A mouth that stretches

The Gans' egg-eater has the largest mouth relative to its size of any snake studied. Biologist Bruce Jayne found very stretchy skin between the left and right lower jaws, which adds more than 50 percent to its gape area.

## No teeth to crack the shell

The teeth are greatly reduced, so the snake cannot bite through a shell. Instead, it swallows the egg whole and uses powerful body contractions in the neck to crush it.

## Spikes on the backbone

Downward-pointing spines on several vertebrae (the 29th to 38th in X-ray studies) project into the throat and rupture the egg membrane, so the contents can flow into the stomach.

## The shell comes back out

Once the egg is emptied, the snake spits the crushed shell back out as a compact bundle.

## The takeaway

No teeth and no venom — yet it swallows an egg wider than its head and cracks it from inside.
