Bears get the scary reputation. But according to the Alaska Department of Fish and Game, more people are injured in Alaska by moose than by bears. How does a plant-eater manage that?

## First, the warning

A moose usually tells you before it attacks. Its ears drop and lie flat back, and the long hair on its hump rises. Anchorage's parks department lists pinned-back ears, raised hackles and a lowered head as signs to back away.

## No bite, only hooves

Unlike a bear, a moose does not bite. It kicks, most often with its front legs. A biologist quoted by Fish and Game says moose "can pretty much kick 360 degrees around them", and a hard kick from a hind leg can kill.

## Rearing up

A moose standing normally is six to seven feet tall at the shoulder. When it rears up, it can reach about nine feet, then bring its hooves down onto a person's head or shoulders.

## When it happens

Mothers defend their calves, and moose are touchier in winter, when they are hungry, tired and struggling in deep snow. Dogs, traffic and people who crowd them can all set them off.

## The takeaway

It never bites, and it has no claws to speak of, yet in Alaska the moose injures more people than the bear.
