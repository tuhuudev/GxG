In 1918, a leopard killed a villager near Rudraprayag, in the hills of northern India. It was the first recorded victim, and the leopard kept going.

## The pilgrim road

Rudraprayag sits on the road to the Hindu shrines of Kedarnath and Badrinath. As the killings continued, people became afraid to walk that road alone at night.

## A region indoors after dark

In the villages, few people left their houses after dark.

## Everything failed

Units of Gurkha and British soldiers were sent to track the leopard, and failed. Gin traps failed. Poison failed. Hunters wounded it, but the wounds healed and the killings went on.

## Jim Corbett

In the autumn of 1925, the hunter and author Jim Corbett took on the leopard. After a ten-week hunt, he shot it on 2 May 1926. It turned out to be a large, elderly male in fine condition apart from a few healed injuries.

## The count

Official records say the leopard killed more than 125 people over about eight years. Corbett believed the real number was higher.

## The takeaway

One leopard, eight years, over 125 people, and a pilgrim road people feared to walk after dark.
