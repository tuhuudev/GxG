One tiger holds the Guinness World Record for the most fatalities in tiger attacks. The likely reason is a small injury inside her mouth.

## 436 people

The Champawat tiger, a Bengal tigress, is credited with about 436 deaths in Nepal and the Kumaon region of India in the late 1800s and early 1900s. Some historians question how exact that figure is, but it is the number most sources cite.

## Two broken fangs

After her death, an examination found that the upper and lower canine teeth on the right side of her mouth were broken: the upper one in half, the lower one down to the bone. The injury is usually blamed on an earlier encounter with a hunter, though that part is a hypothesis rather than a recorded fact.

## Why people?

Jim Corbett, who later shot her, argued that the damaged teeth left her unable to hunt her normal wild prey, so she turned to people, who were slower and easier to kill. Later examination of man-eating tigers has given weight to that idea.

## Driven across a border

She was driven out of Nepal by the Nepalese army and crossed into Kumaon, India, where the killings continued.

## 1907

Her final victim was a 16-year-old girl, Premka Devi, near the town of Champawat. Jim Corbett tracked the tigress and shot her in 1907.

## The takeaway

Two broken teeth may explain how one tiger became the deadliest man-eater on record.
