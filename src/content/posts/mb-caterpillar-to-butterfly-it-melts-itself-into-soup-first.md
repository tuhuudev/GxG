A caterpillar does not simply grow wings inside its chrysalis. First, it digests its own body.

## The meltdown

Once the chrysalis forms, the caterpillar releases enzymes that break down most of its own tissues. If you cut a chrysalis open at the right moment, caterpillar soup would ooze out.

## What survives

Not everything dissolves. Small clusters of cells called imaginal discs survive the breakdown. The caterpillar grew them while it was still developing inside its egg — one disc for each adult body part it will need.

## Rebuilding from the soup

With the rest of the body broken down, the imaginal discs use the protein-rich soup around them as fuel. They divide rapidly and build the adult's wings, legs, eyes and other structures. Depending on the species, some caterpillar muscles and parts of the nervous system are largely kept and carried into the adult.

## The takeaway

A caterpillar dissolves its own body, then rebuilds it with wings — from a set of blueprints it has carried since before it hatched.
