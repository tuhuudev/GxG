# The Kill Is Only Half The Job

A lion kills and eats on the ground, backed by a pride. A leopard hunts alone — and on the African savanna, anything left on the ground gets stolen. Hyenas and lions will take a leopard's kill if they can reach it.

So the leopard does something no other big cat does routinely: it takes the whole carcass up a tree.

## The Choke

Leopards kill larger prey by suffocation. The cat anchors itself with its claws, clamps its jaws around the throat, and simply holds — cutting off the airway while the animal struggles — until the kicking stops.

## The Climb

Then comes the part that looks impossible. With retractable claws gripping bark like grappling hooks and enormous shoulder and neck strength, the leopard drags the dead animal up the trunk and wedges it in a fork several metres off the ground.

Researchers describing this "tree lardering" behaviour report that leopards hoist kills weighing anywhere from about 40% to 140% of their own body mass. In other words, the carcass can outweigh the cat carrying it.

## Why Bother

Hyenas cannot climb. Lions can, but poorly, and rarely follow. A hoisted kill is safe from scavengers, which lets the leopard return and feed undisturbed for several days instead of losing the meal within minutes.

## The Loop

One choke, one climb, and the meal hangs where no thief can reach it.
