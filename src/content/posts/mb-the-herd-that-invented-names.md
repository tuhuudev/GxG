# Rumbles With Addresses

Elephants talk in rumbles — low, rolling calls that carry across the savanna. For decades, researchers assumed these were broadcasts: greetings, warnings, contact calls meant for whoever was listening.

A study published in *Nature Ecology & Evolution* in June 2024 suggests something far stranger. Some of those rumbles have addresses. They are meant for one particular elephant — something very close to a name.

## Teaching a Computer to Read the Mail

The work was led by behavioral ecologist Michael Pardo of Colorado State University, together with Save the Elephants, drawing on 14 months of fieldwork spread over four years in Kenya — around Amboseli National Park and the Samburu and Buffalo Springs reserves.

The team analyzed 469 rumbles involving 101 calling elephants and 117 receivers. They fed the recordings to a machine-learning model and asked it a simple question: from the sound alone, can you tell who this call was for? The model identified the intended receiver better than chance — meaning the calls carried individually specific, name-like acoustic information.

## The Speaker Test

Statistics can mislead, so the researchers ran the obvious real-world check. They played recordings through a hidden speaker to 17 wild elephants.

The result was clean: elephants responded to calls that had originally been addressed to them — approaching, calling back — and largely ignored calls addressed to other elephants, even when those calls came from the same caller. Each animal picked its own name out of the air.

## Not an Imitation — an Invention

Here is the detail that separates elephants from almost every other animal. Dolphins and parrots can target an individual, but they do it by imitating the receiver's own signature sound — like getting someone's attention by impersonating them.

The elephant calls showed no such imitation. The "name" is not a copy of the receiver's voice; it appears to be an arbitrary label the callers use for that individual. Arbitrary labels for specific individuals is precisely how human names work — and that ability had never been convincingly shown in a wild animal before.

## What Else Is in the Rumble?

If elephants have invented one kind of word, the natural question is what else their rumbles encode — and researchers suspect the answer is: much more than we currently understand.

Somewhere on the savanna right now, an elephant is calling a name. We are only beginning to learn how to hear it.
