A starfish has no brain and no blood. It still eats clams and mussels — by sending its stomach out of its body to do the work.

## Prying the shell

The starfish wraps its arms around a clam or mussel and grips the shell with thousands of tiny tube feet. Then it pulls. The bivalve clamps shut, but the starfish can keep pulling for hours, even days, until the shell muscle tires and the shell opens slightly.

## A stomach through a one-millimetre gap

It does not need much room. A gap of about one millimetre is enough for the starfish to push one of its stomachs out through its mouth and into the shell.

## Digestion outside the body

Inside the shell, the everted stomach releases digestive enzymes that break the soft tissue down into liquid. When the meal is done, the starfish pulls its stomach back in and leaves the empty shell behind.

## The takeaway

No brain, no blood — and it digests its prey inside the prey's own shell.
