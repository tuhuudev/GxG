# Africa Calls It the Black Death

Lions get the reputation. Cape buffalo get the body count. Across Africa, this one-ton bovine is widely held responsible for more human deaths each year than lions are — a figure commonly cited at around 200 people annually.

## A Skull Plate Built Like Armour

An adult bull can weigh up to a ton, and the base of its horns fuses across the forehead into a solid plate of bone known as the boss. The horns sweep down and then hook upward, which means a buffalo does not simply push an opponent aside — it drives upward with the tips, through whatever is in front of it.

## Thirty-Five Miles an Hour, Straight Ahead

Cape buffalo can charge at speeds reported up to about 35 miles per hour. That is roughly the pace of a car on a residential street, carrying the mass of a small car, with two hooked spikes at the front. There is very little a person on foot can do about it.

## The Behaviour That Earned the Nickname

What genuinely separates the Cape buffalo from other large, aggressive herbivores is not the charge itself but what a wounded one does afterward. A buffalo that has been shot and not killed is known to circle back on its own trail and wait for whoever is following it. Professional hunters treat a wounded buffalo as one of the most dangerous situations in the field precisely because it turns the pursuit around.

That habit is where the nickname comes from. "Black Death" is not about how the animal looks. It is about the fact that it does not simply flee — it comes back.
