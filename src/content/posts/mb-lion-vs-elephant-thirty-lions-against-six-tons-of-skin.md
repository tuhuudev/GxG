An adult African elephant should be untouchable. Yet in one corner of Botswana, a single pride of lions turned elephants into regular prey.

## Armour that starts at the skin

African elephant skin is roughly 2–4 cm thick across most of the body, and up to about 3.8 cm — around an inch and a half — in places. Underneath sits an animal that can weigh close to six tons.

## Outweighing a whole pride

A large male lion weighs around 190 kg. A full-grown bull elephant outweighs about thirty of them put together — roughly the size of the pride that hunts them.

## The Savuti pride

A study by Power and Compion in *African Zoology* (2009) followed lions in the Savuti, Chobe National Park. In the late dry season (August to November), when migratory prey has left, the pride of about thirty lions switched to elephants and killed one roughly every three days.

## Choosing the night, and the young

Most kills happened after dark. And the lions did not take on giant bulls: seven of the eight elephants documented in the study were between four and eleven years old — big, but not yet full-grown.

## The takeaway

Six tons of skin and muscle is not enough on its own. Thirty lions, darkness and a dry season changed the odds.
