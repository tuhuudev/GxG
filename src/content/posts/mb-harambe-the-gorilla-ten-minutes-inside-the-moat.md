On May 28, 2016, a young boy climbed through a barrier at the Gorilla World exhibit of the Cincinnati Zoo and fell into the moat below. A 17-year-old western lowland silverback named Harambe went down to him.

## A silverback and a child

Harambe weighed over 400 pounds. Over the next minutes he handled the boy and dragged him through the shallow water of the moat while visitors watched from above.

## About ten minutes

Zoo staff had roughly ten minutes to decide how to get the boy out alive.

## Why not a tranquilizer?

Zoo director Thane Maynard explained that a tranquilizer dart does not drop an animal instantly. It takes minutes to work, and the sting of the dart could agitate the gorilla in the meantime, with the child still in his hands.

## One shot

The zoo's response team killed Harambe with a single rifle shot. According to firefighters at the scene, the boy was sitting between the gorilla's legs when the shot was fired. He was taken to hospital and was expected to recover.

## Was Harambe protecting him?

The decision is still debated. Primatologist Jane Goodall wrote to the zoo's director that, in parts of the footage, Harambe seemed protective of the boy, and compared it to a gorilla at Brookfield Zoo that cradled a child in 1996. She also agreed that a tranquilizer would have taken too long.

## The takeaway

A boy in a gorilla moat, ten minutes to decide, and an argument that has never really ended.
