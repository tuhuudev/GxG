The classic image of the Great Migration is a crocodile lunging at a wildebeest in the Mara River. The bigger killer at the crossing is the river itself.

## Over 6,000 drownings a year

A 2017 study led by Amanda Subalusky of the Cary Institute estimated that an average of about 6,250 wildebeest drown in the Mara River every year during the migration.

## How it happens

Wildebeest cross in huge numbers, jumping from steep banks into the water. Many never make it out the other side.

## Crocodiles can't keep up

Crocodiles do take some of the animals, but they can only eat so much. Only a small portion of the drowned bodies are eaten by crocodiles.

## 1,100 tonnes in one river

The drownings add roughly 1,100 tonnes of biomass to the river each year, which the researchers compared to about ten blue whales.

## Seven years of bones

Soft tissue breaks down in a few weeks, but the bones take around seven years to decay, slowly releasing nutrients into the river ecosystem.

## The takeaway

Crocodiles wait at every wildebeest crossing, but the river kills far more.
