The Nile crocodile is one of the most dangerous predators in Africa, responsible for hundreds of human deaths every year. It shares its rivers with the hippopotamus — and when the two meet, it's often the crocodile that gets out of the way.

## A bite of five thousand pounds

The bite force of an adult Nile crocodile has been measured by Brady Barr at about 22 kilonewtons — roughly 5,000 pounds of force. It's one of the strongest bites ever recorded in a living animal.

## Armour made of skin

A hippo's skin is around 6 centimetres (2.4 inches) thick across much of its body. Under it is a huge body: adult hippos average about 1.4 to 1.5 tonnes — around a ton and a half.

## Jaws and teeth

Hippos can open their mouths to 100–110 degrees, wider than a right angle. Inside are teeth that keep sharpening against each other, with the largest growing up to 50 centimetres (about 20 inches).

## Who backs down

Very large Nile crocodiles have been seen occasionally preying on hippo calves, half-grown hippos and possibly adult females. But crocodiles are also frequent targets of hippo aggression: because they live in the same stretches of river, crocodiles may be chased off or even killed by hippos.

## The takeaway

Five thousand pounds of bite is enough for almost anything in the river. For an adult hippo, the crocodile usually moves aside.
