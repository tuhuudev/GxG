A wolf pack is one of the most efficient hunting teams in North America. So what happens when a single grizzly walks up to its kill?

## A kill every couple of days

A 2017 study in *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* (Tallian and colleagues) followed wolves in Yellowstone National Park and in Scandinavia. In Yellowstone's summer, the average time between one wolf kill and the next was about 2.2 days.

## The bear shows up

The study notes that Yellowstone's brown bears frequently take carcasses from wolves. Bear sign was found at 45% of large ungulate kills made by wolves (105 of 232), and at 27% of all summer kills.

## The wolves wait

You might expect wolves that lose a carcass to go and hunt again sooner. The data showed the opposite. In Yellowstone, a bear at a kill was linked to a 7.6-hour longer wait before the pack's next kill, about 14% longer. The authors suggest wolves may linger at a usurped carcass, waiting for chances to feed while the bear is there.

## Two continents, same pattern

In Scandinavia, wolf packs that shared their territory with bears killed less often than packs without bears; in summer the interval grew by about 28%.

## The takeaway

One grizzly can reset a whole wolf pack's hunting clock.
