In the 1990s, something was killing white rhinos in Pilanesberg National Park, South Africa. The killers were not poachers. They were young elephants.

## The killings

Researchers Rob Slotow and Gus van Dyk estimated that up to 49 rhinos were killed by elephants in Pilanesberg, with confirmed kills in 1994 and 1996. Science News put the count at more than 40 white rhinos in five years.

## Orphans with no elders

The young males had been moved to the park as orphans from culls in Kruger National Park. Pilanesberg had no mature bulls, so there was no adult male hierarchy to keep them in check.

## Musth, years too early

Musth is a period of heightened testosterone and aggression in male elephants. The study found the young Pilanesberg males entered musth about ten years younger than expected — while still teenagers by elephant standards.

## The fix

In 1998, six full-grown bull elephants were introduced. The older bulls shortened the time the young males spent in musth, and rhino deaths returned to normal. Other reserves, such as Hluhluwe-iMfolozi, later used Pilanesberg as the model for introducing older bulls.

## The takeaway

Forty-plus white rhinos died because there was no adult elephant around to keep the young ones in line.
