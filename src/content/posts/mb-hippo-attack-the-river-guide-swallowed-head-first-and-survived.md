On March 9, 1996, river guide Paul Templer was leading a canoe safari on the Zambezi River when a hippo erupted from the water and swallowed him head first.

## Up to his waist

Templer later described being inside the hippo's mouth up to his waist. Contemporary reports put the animal at around 4,000 pounds.

## It came back

The hippo did not attack just once. It took him under, released him, and struck again; a 1996 Seattle Times report described the guide surviving an animal that hit him three times.

## The rescue

Another guide reached Templer and got him to a rock in the middle of the river, where he could be pulled out of the water.

## The cost

An apprentice guide in another canoe, Evans Namasango, drowned in the attack. Templer survived with severe injuries, and surgeons later amputated his left arm at the shoulder.

## A grass eater

What makes the story stranger: hippos eat mostly grass. Their attacks are about territory and defence, not hunger.

## The takeaway

A grass eater swallowed a man head first, and he lived to tell it.
