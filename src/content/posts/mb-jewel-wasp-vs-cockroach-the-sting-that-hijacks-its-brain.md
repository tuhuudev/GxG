The emerald jewel wasp (*Ampulex compressa*) is smaller than the cockroach it hunts, roughly half its size. Yet it does not kill the roach or drag it by force. Instead, it stings the cockroach in the brain, and the cockroach walks to its own grave.

## Step one: a sting that freezes the legs

The wasp grabs the cockroach and hooks her abdomen underneath it. The first sting goes in just below the head and temporarily paralyses the roach's front legs, so it cannot fight back.

## Step two: a sting inside the brain

With the legs out of action, the wasp delivers a second, far more precise sting directly into the cockroach's brain. Researchers at Ben-Gurion University found that this venom does not paralyse the roach. It removes something subtler: the cockroach's motivation to walk on its own. It can still move, but it no longer tries to escape.

## Step three: the antenna leash

The wasp then bites off part of one of the roach's antennae and drinks a little of its fluid from the wound. Gripping the stump, she guides the cockroach into her burrow, leading it like a dog on a leash. The roach follows without a struggle.

## Step four: eaten alive in the dark

Inside the burrow the wasp lays a single egg on the cockroach's underside, then leaves and seals the entrance. When the egg hatches, the larva chews into the roach and feeds on it, first from outside and then from within, over roughly a week, keeping the host alive while it grows. Eventually a new adult wasp emerges from the cockroach's body.

## The takeaway

The cockroach is bigger than the wasp. It never gets dragged anywhere. After one sting in the brain, it walks into its grave by itself.
