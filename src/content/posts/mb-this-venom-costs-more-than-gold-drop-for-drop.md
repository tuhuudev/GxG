# A Sting Worth More Than Gold

The deathstalker scorpion, found across North Africa and the Middle East, is widely considered the most venomous scorpion on Earth. Its venom is a cocktail of neurotoxins potent enough to cause intense pain, muscle paralysis, and — in vulnerable people — respiratory failure from just a small dose delivered through its sting.

## A Tiny Drop Goes a Long Way

The venom works by targeting the channels nerve cells use to send electrical signals, disrupting normal nerve transmission almost immediately after a sting. That is exactly why so little of it is needed to produce such an outsized effect on a nervous system.

## The Strangest Part Isn't the Danger

Here's the twist that has nothing to do with how deadly it is: deathstalker venom is widely cited as the most expensive liquid in the world, with pricing estimates around 39 million dollars per gallon. Extracting it is slow, painstaking work — scorpions produce only tiny amounts at a time, and milking them safely takes specialized equipment and expertise — which is a big part of why the price climbs so high.

## Why Would Anyone Want It?

Part of the value comes from research interest rather than the venom's danger. Some of its components have been studied for potential uses in areas like cancer imaging and other medical research, which keeps demand — and price — elevated even though production stays tiny. None of that changes what the venom does on contact: it remains one of the most potent stings on the planet, priced like a precious metal and behaving like anything but.
