Stingrays are armed with a venomous barb on the tail. For the great hammerhead, that barb is barely an inconvenience.

## The favourite prey

Stingrays are a major part of the great hammerhead's diet. Researchers have watched hammerheads strike a ray from above, drive it down against the seabed, and hold it there.

## A head that works like a hand

The hammerhead's wide, flattened head is used to pin the ray in place. With the ray trapped, the shark pivots and bites pieces off its wings one at a time.

## Barbs in the mouth

The rays fight back with their barbs, and many end up lodged in the shark. Shark researcher Perry Gilbert reported one large hammerhead caught off Florida with 96 stingray barbs embedded in its jaws, mouth and head — and the shark seemed largely unbothered. In a more recent Florida case, a 12-foot great hammerhead was examined with a stingray barb lodged in its jaw.

## The takeaway

A single stingray barb has, in rare cases, killed a human. One hammerhead reportedly carried ninety-six of them.
