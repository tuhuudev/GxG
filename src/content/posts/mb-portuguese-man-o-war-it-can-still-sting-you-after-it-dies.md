That blue, balloon-like float on the sand looks harmless. Beach officials keep warning people not to touch it, even when it is clearly dead.

## More than a float

The float is only the part you see. Below it, the man o' war's tentacles are usually a few feet long but can grow to around 100 feet.

## Loaded tentacles

The tentacles are covered in stinging cells. Each cell holds a coiled, venom-loaded thread that fires when touched. The cells fire on contact, without any brain telling them to.

## Still stinging on the beach

Because the stinging cells work on their own, they can keep firing after the animal is dead and washed ashore. Reports describe stings from stranded man o' wars days, and even weeks, after they wash up. On human skin, stings typically leave painful red welts.

## Not one animal

Although it looks like a jellyfish, the Portuguese man o' war is a siphonophore: a colony of specialised units called zooids or polyps that work together as one organism.

## The takeaway

A dead man o' war on the beach is still a whole colony of armed stingers. Look, don't touch.
