# A Weapon Faster Than A Fighter Jet

A chameleon looks like the least athletic animal in the forest — it walks in slow motion, rocking back and forth like it's stalling for time. Then it fires its tongue, and for a fraction of a second it becomes one of the fastest-accelerating things in the animal kingdom.

## The Real Shock Comes After the Launch

The slow walk is the misdirection. The tongue itself launches at accelerations measured up to roughly 264 times the pull of gravity — figures that, gram for gram, outperform a fighter jet catapulting off a carrier deck. A human pilot pulling that kind of sustained acceleration would black out long before a chameleon's tongue even finishes its strike.

## Two Hundredths of a Second, Start to Finish

The entire event — launch, extension, contact, and retraction — is over in about 20 milliseconds. That's roughly the length of a single frame in a slow-motion nature documentary, and far faster than a human eye can consciously track without the help of high-speed cameras.

## A Built-In Slingshot, Not a Muscle

The mechanism behind this isn't raw muscle power — chameleon tongues work more like a loaded crossbow. Elastic tissue inside the tongue gets compressed and held under tension by the surrounding muscle, storing energy the way a stretched rubber band does. When released, that stored energy launches the tongue explosively, letting even small chameleon species hit a target more than twice their own body length away.

It's easy to watch a chameleon amble along a branch and assume nothing fast lives inside that body. The tongue says otherwise — faster than a jet, and gone before you'd even finish a blink.
