Lions are Africa's top land predators, and their usual way of killing is brutally direct: a bite that clamps onto the throat. Against most prey, that works. Against an adult giraffe, the throat is simply out of reach — and getting close enough to try can be fatal.

## A throat eighteen feet up

Fully grown giraffes stand between 4.3 and 5.7 metres tall (14 to 19 feet), with males the tallest. An adult male averages about 1,200 kilograms. That height puts the giraffe's throat far above anything a lion can reach from the ground.

## Hooves the size of dinner plates

A giraffe's main defence is its legs. The hooves of large males reach about 31 by 23 centimetres — roughly the size of a dinner plate. According to wildlife experts quoted by National Geographic, an adult male giraffe can kill a lion with a single kick. It's one reason adult giraffes are mostly safe from predation, with lions as their only major threat.

## How lions try anyway

Because a direct attack is so dangerous, lions that do take on an adult giraffe work as a group. Experts describe lions climbing on top of the giraffe together, trying to push it down with sheer weight.

## The Kruger attack

In Kruger National Park in South Africa, a rare video showed exactly this. One lion clambered onto a giraffe's back while two others wrapped themselves around his legs. The giraffe shook them off within about five minutes — and kept them at bay for hours afterwards by trying to stomp them.

## The takeaway

A lion kills at the throat. A giraffe keeps its throat eighteen feet up, carries hooves that can kill, and can fight off a pride for hours.
