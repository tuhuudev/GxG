In October 2005, researchers in Everglades National Park found one of the most famous predator photos ever taken: a dead Burmese python with an alligator sticking out of its body.

## A meal too big

The python was about 13 feet long. Inside it was a six-foot American alligator. The snake's midsection had split open, and its head was missing.

## What actually happened?

Nobody knows for certain. The most common explanation is that the meal was simply too much for the snake. A University of Florida biologist suggested the alligator may have clawed at the python's stomach. Others have pointed to gases from the rotting meal, or to something removing the snake's head after death.

## A jaw built to stretch

Snakes can swallow huge prey because the two halves of their lower jaw are joined by stretchy soft tissue rather than fused bone, and the skin between them expands enormously.

## The deer record

In a case described in 2024, a 14.8-foot female Burmese python in Florida swallowed a white-tailed deer weighing 76.9 pounds — about two thirds of the snake's own body mass. Its mouth stretched to about 93% of its estimated maximum gape.

## The takeaway

A python can swallow an alligator whole. Whether it survives the meal is another question.
