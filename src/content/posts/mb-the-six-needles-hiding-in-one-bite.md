# Not a Needle — a Toolkit

Under a microscope, the mosquito's mouthpart is not the single tube it appears to be. The proboscis is a sheath, and inside it ride six separate needle-like instruments called stylets. When a female mosquito lands on you, she deploys all six in sequence — a full surgical kit, small enough to work without waking you.

## The Saw Blades

Two of the stylets, the maxillae, are edged like tiny serrated knives. They vibrate as they cut, sawing a passage through the skin with far less force than a single push would need — one reason the entry is so hard to feel.

## The Retractors

Two more stylets, the mandibles, work like the forks a surgeon uses to hold an incision open. They brace your skin apart so the cutting blades can keep working deeper.

## The Probe That Hunts by Taste

The fifth instrument, the labrum, is the drinking tube — and it is not passive. Equipped with receptors, it bends and probes beneath the skin, *tasting* its way toward a blood vessel until it strikes a vein.

## The Spit That Betrays You Twice

The sixth stylet, the hypopharynx, injects saliva. That saliva carries a numbing agent — which is why you feel nothing during the bite — plus an anticoagulant and a vessel dilator that keep your blood flowing freely while she drinks.

You only find out later. The bump and the maddening itch are your immune system reacting to the saliva she left behind. The itch, quite literally, is her leftover spit.
