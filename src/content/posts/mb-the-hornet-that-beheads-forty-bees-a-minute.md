# A Raid Measured In Heads

The Asian giant hornet (*Vespa mandarinia*) is the largest hornet on Earth — workers reach about 45 mm, queens close to 50 mm. Its weapon against honeybees is not the sting. It is the jaw. A single hornet can kill roughly forty bees a minute, usually by biting the head clean off or crushing the body with its mandibles.

That is one hornet. A raiding party is worse.

## Thirty Hornets, One Dead Hive

A scout finds a hive and marks it with a pheromone. Then a few dozen hornets return together. Sources describing these "slaughter phase" raids report that about thirty hornets can destroy a full-strength colony — tens of thousands of bees — in a matter of hours, sometimes in around ninety minutes. The hornets carry the severed thoraxes, the protein-rich flight muscle, back to feed their own larvae.

## A Sting Built For Suits

The sting itself is about 6 mm — a quarter inch — long enough to punch through an ordinary beekeeping suit. The venom is a cocktail of neurotoxins and tissue-destroying compounds; it is strong enough to damage human skin tissue, and in Japan it is linked to dozens of deaths a year, mostly from multiple stings or allergic reactions.

## The Bees' Living Oven

European honeybees have no defence and are simply wiped out. Japanese honeybees (*Apis cerana japonica*) evolved one.

When a hornet scout enters the hive, hundreds of workers — more than five hundred in studied cases — pile onto it and form a tight sphere called a **hot defensive bee ball**. Inside, the bees vibrate their flight muscles. The temperature at the core climbs to roughly 46–47 °C, and carbon dioxide builds up in the ball. That is lethal for the hornet within about half an hour to an hour.

It is only a few degrees below the point that would kill the bees themselves — which is why they survive the oven they build.

## The Loop

A jaw that takes forty heads a minute, defeated by animals it could kill one at a time — because five hundred of them turned their own bodies into a furnace.
