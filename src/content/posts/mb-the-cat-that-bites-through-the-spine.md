# One Bite Between the Neck Bones

The mountain lion — also called cougar or puma — is the widest-ranging wild cat in the Americas, and one of the most efficient ambush predators on the continent. Its signature is a kill so precise it looks surgical: a bite aimed between the vertebrae of the neck.

## Prey Twice Its Weight

An adult mountain lion typically weighs somewhere in the range of a grown man. Its preferred prey, deer and elk, can weigh far more — a cow elk can be roughly twice the mass of the cat. The lion closes that gap with technique rather than brute size.

## A Forty Foot Leap From Behind

Mountain lions hunt by stalking close and then launching. They can leap forward as much as 40 feet in a single bound and clear a 12 foot fence, and they are widely reported to jump around 15 feet straight up. The attack comes from behind, landing on the back of the animal before it has a chance to react.

## The Kill Bite

Wildlife forensics from mountain lion kill sites describe two outcomes. On deer, the cat often delivers a bite that breaks the neck or penetrates the skull, positioning its teeth between the vertebrae and into the spinal cord. On larger ungulates, it may instead clamp the throat and crush the larynx, suffocating the animal. Either way, the bite is aimed with precision at the structures that end a struggle fastest.

## Silent and Rarely Seen

Mountain lions are secretive, and most people who share a landscape with them never see one. That combination — a man-sized cat, a 40 foot leap, and a bite engineered for the spine — is what makes it one of the most effective hunters in North America, and why park services urge visitors to stay alert on trails at dawn and dusk.
