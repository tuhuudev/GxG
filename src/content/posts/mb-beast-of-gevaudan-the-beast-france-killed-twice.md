On June 30, 1764, a teenage girl named Jeanne Boulet was killed near Langogne, in the Gévaudan region of southern France. Her burial record blamed "the beast". She was the first officially recorded victim of the Beast of Gévaudan.

## Three years of attacks

Between 1764 and 1767 the attacks kept coming. Estimates vary by source, but around 100 people are usually said to have been killed, many of them women and children working alone in the fields.

## What witnesses saw

Descriptions varied, but most witnesses described something that looked like a wolf, about the size of a calf.

## The king's hunters

The attacks became a national story, and King Louis XV sent professional hunters, followed by dragoons. In September 1765 François Antoine, the king's gun-bearer, shot a very large wolf. It was stuffed and taken to the royal court, and the case was considered closed.

## It came back

Within months, the attacks started again.

## The second beast

On June 19, 1767, a local hunter named Jean Chastel shot another large animal. After that, the killings stopped. Popular stories say he used a silver bullet, but that detail is legend, not documented fact.

## So what was it?

Two animals were shot, and the true identity of the Beast is still unknown. Proposed explanations include unusually large wolves, wolf-dog hybrids, or even an escaped exotic animal. None has been proven.

## The takeaway

A beast killed around 100 people, two were shot, and nobody knows what it really was.
