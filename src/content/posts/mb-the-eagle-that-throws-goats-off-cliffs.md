# The Bird That Drags Goats Off Cliffs

The golden eagle is one of the largest raptors in the Northern Hemisphere, with a wingspan that can pass two metres. But its most notorious hunting trick is not about size at all. It is about physics: an eagle that cannot possibly carry a mountain goat can still kill one, simply by taking it over the edge.

## A Bird Versus Ten Times Its Weight

Golden eagles typically prey on rabbits, hares, marmots and ground squirrels — animals they can lift and carry away. Yet they are also documented hunting far larger prey, including young deer, bighorn sheep and mountain goats. An adult mountain goat can weigh many times more than the eagle itself, which makes a conventional "grab and fly" attack impossible.

## The 150 Mile Per Hour Dive

The attack begins with speed. Golden eagles dive on prey at more than 150 miles per hour, among the fastest stoops of any bird. That momentum, combined with talons built to pierce and hold, is enough to hit a goat standing on a narrow ledge hard.

## Letting Gravity Finish the Job

Video footage from mountain ranges in North America and Europe shows the sequence: the eagle sinks its talons into the back of a goat or chamois on a cliff, then hauls or drags it over the edge. The bird does not need to lift the animal — it only needs to move it a few feet sideways. The fall of hundreds of feet does the killing, and the eagle glides down to feed at the bottom.

## Why It Works

The strategy sidesteps the two hardest parts of hunting large prey: subduing an animal that could injure the bird, and transporting a carcass far heavier than the hunter. By using the terrain, the golden eagle turns a cliff into a weapon. It is a reminder that the most dangerous hunters are not always the strongest — sometimes they are the ones that understand where they are standing.
